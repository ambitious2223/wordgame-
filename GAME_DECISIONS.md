# TikTok Word Game - Interactive Decision Cards

## How to Use
Each card presents a critical decision with detailed options. Choose one per card to build your game design document.

# CARD 1: Core Game Format

## What format should your word game take?

### Option A: Anagram Scramble (RECOMMENDED)
**Description:** Letters are scrambled and players unscramble them to form words. Classic Wheel of Fortune style. Letters like "TACOH" → "CHOAT", "COAT", "CHAT", "OATH". Simple to understand, universally recognized format. Creates "aha!" moments when players crack the code. Works perfectly for short TikTok attention spans.

**Pros:** Instantly understood, high satisfaction on solve, great for all ages
**Cons:** Can feel dated if not freshened up
**Best for:** Broad appeal, quick sessions, family-friendly content

### Option B: Letter Pool Builder
**Description:** Players see a pool of available letters and must build words using them (like Scrabble without the board). Example: Letters "A, E, T, R, S" → "STAR", "RATES", "STARE", "TEARS". Multiple words possible per round. Encourages vocabulary and speed.

**Pros:** Multiple solutions, strategic depth, longer engagement per round
**Cons:** More complex, harder to verify answers quickly
**Best for:** Word game enthusiasts, competitive play

### Option C: Missing Letter Fill
**Description:** Word shown with blanks, players guess missing letters. Example: "S _ _ R D" → "SWORD". Combines word knowledge with letter recognition. Builds tension as letters reveal.

**Pros:** Guided guessing, educational value, clear win state
**Cons:** Less creative freedom, can be solved by brute force
**Best for:** Educational content, casual players

### Option D: Category Word Chain
**Description:** Letters provided, players must guess words from a specific category. Example: Letters "A, P, L, E" with category "Fruit" → "APPLE". Combines lateral thinking with vocabulary.

**Pros:** Themed content, easier to create viral challenges
**Cons:** Category bias, limited word options
**Best for:** Themed content, specific audiences

# CARD 2: Letter Count Per Round

## How many letters should each round provide?

### Option A: 5 Letters (RECOMMENDED)
**Description:** Sweet spot for TikTok. Quick to process, multiple word options typically 3-5 valid words. Matches Wordle's successful formula. Players can mentally process in 2-3 seconds. Perfect for 15-20 second rounds.

**Pros:** Fast rounds, high completion rate, mobile-friendly
**Cons:** Limited word variety, less challenge
**Best for:** Casual players, high engagement, short sessions

### Option B: 6 Letters
**Description:** Slightly more challenging. Opens up more word possibilities (typically 5-8 valid words). Still manageable in quick rounds. Good balance of challenge and accessibility.

**Pros:** More word options, moderate challenge, good variety
**Cons:** Slightly longer processing time
**Best for:** Regular players, balanced gameplay

### Option C: 7 Letters
**Description:** Significantly harder. More "master words" possible. Requires stronger vocabulary. Creates impressive "wow" moments when solved quickly. Can feel intimidating for casual players.

**Pros:** High skill ceiling, impressive solves, deep gameplay
**Cons:** Steeper learning curve, casual player exclusion
**Best for:** Competitive play, word game experts

### Option D: Variable (Progressive Difficulty)
**Description:** Start with 5 letters, increase as game progresses. Round 1-3: 5 letters, Round 4-6: 6 letters, Round 7+: 7 letters. Maintains accessibility while adding challenge.

**Pros:** Onboarding-friendly, progressive challenge, longer sessions
**Cons:** More complex to implement, inconsistent experience
**Best for:** Long-form content, tournament play

# CARD 3: Round Time Limit

## How long should players have to guess?

### Option A: 15 Seconds (RECOMMENDED)
**Description:** Ultra-fast, TikTok-native pace. Creates urgency and excitement. Perfect for live interaction where comments fly fast. Matches typical TikTok video length psychology. High energy, high pressure.

**Pros:** Matches TikTok attention span, creates excitement, high energy
**Cons:** May frustrate slower players, less time for complex words
**Best for:** Live streams, high-energy content, casual players

### Option B: 20 Seconds
**Description:** Slightly more breathing room. Still fast but allows for thinking. Good balance between pressure and fairness. Players can process and type without panic.

**Pros:** More fair, allows thinking time, less stressful
**Cons:** Slightly less exciting, slower pace
**Best for:** Regular play, mixed skill levels

### Option C: 30 Seconds
**Description:** Standard game show timing. Allows for careful consideration. Better for longer words or complex rounds. More relaxing pace but less TikTok-optimized.

**Pros:** Fair for all skill levels, reduces frustration
**Cons:** Slower pace, less TikTok-native
**Best for:** Tournament play, educational content

### Option D: Variable by Difficulty
**Description:** Easy rounds: 25 seconds, Medium: 20 seconds, Hard: 15 seconds. Matches time to challenge level. More complex to implement but fairer.

**Pros:** Fair difficulty scaling, adaptive experience
**Cons:** Complexity, inconsistent pacing
**Best for:** Skill-based matchmaking, progressive games

# CARD 4: Player Guess Submission Method

## How do players submit their guesses?

### Option A: TikTok LIVE Comments (RECOMMENDED)
**Description:** Players type guesses in the LIVE chat/comments. Most accessible - no extra apps needed. Integrates naturally with TikTok behavior. You read and validate guesses in real-time. High engagement, visible to all viewers.

**Pros:** Zero friction, native TikTok behavior, maximum participation
**Cons:** Hard to track individual players, spam potential, verification difficulty
**Best for:** Maximum reach, casual play, viral potential

### Option B: TikTok LIVE with Hashtag Tracking
**Description:** Players type guesses with a specific hashtag (#WordGuess123). Easier to track and count. Can use third-party tools to monitor. More organized than raw comments.

**Pros:** Better tracking, organized, still accessible
**Cons:** Extra step for players, hashtag fatigue
**Best for:** Organized competitions, data collection

### Option C: External Web App
**Description:** Players join via link in bio, submit guesses through a web interface. Real-time leaderboard, individual tracking, better UX. Requires players to leave TikTok temporarily.

**Pros:** Better UX, real-time tracking, individual scores
**Cons:** Friction to join, loses TikTok context, requires development
**Best for:** Serious competitions, monetization, data

### Option D: Duet/Stitch Challenges
**Description:** Post the puzzle, players create Duet/Stitch videos with their answers. Asynchronous play, creates UGC content. Not real-time but generates viral content.

**Pros:** Creates UGC, viral potential, no live requirement
**Cons:** Not real-time, harder to manage, slower feedback
**Best for:** Content creation, asynchronous challenges, viral growth

# CARD 5: Scoring System

## How should players earn points?

### Option A: Letter Value System (RECOMMENDED)
**Description:** Each letter has a point value (like Scrabble). Common letters (A, E, T) = 1 point, Rare letters (Q, Z, X) = 5-10 points. Word score = sum of letter values. Rewards finding words with rare letters. Strategic depth.

**Pros:** Strategic, rewards vocabulary, familiar system
**Cons:** Requires reference chart, complexity
**Best for:** Competitive play, word game enthusiasts

### Option B: Word Length Multiplier
**Description:** Points = word length × multiplier. 3 letters = 3 points, 4 letters = 8 points (2x), 5 letters = 15 points (3x). Simple to understand, rewards longer words. Easy mental math.

**Pros:** Simple, intuitive, rewards effort
**Cons:** Less strategic, predictable
**Best for:** Casual players, quick understanding

### Option C: Speed + Accuracy Bonus
**Description:** Base points for correct guess + speed bonus for fast answers. First correct guess = 3x points, Second = 2x, Third = 1x. Creates urgency and rewards quick thinking.

**Pros:** Creates excitement, rewards speed, competitive
**Cons:** Can feel unfair to slower players
**Best for:** Live competition, high-energy play

### Option D: Combo/Streak System
**Description:** Consecutive correct guesses increase multiplier. 1st correct = 1x, 2nd = 2x, 3rd = 3x, etc. Missing resets to 1x. Rewards consistency, creates tension.

**Pros:** Rewards consistency, creates drama, long-term strategy
**Cons:** Punishes mistakes heavily, frustrating for casuals
**Best for:** Tournament play, competitive streaks

# CARD 7: Letter Display Style

### Option A: Floating Tiles (RECOMMENDED)
**Description:** Individual letter tiles that float/bounce slightly. Each letter in its own rounded rectangle. 3D effect with shadows. Interactive feel - letters feel "touchable." Creates playful energy. Easy to animate.

**Pros:** Playful, interactive feel, easy to animate, clear separation
**Cons:** Can look childish if overdone

### Option B: Spinning Wheel
**Description:** Letters arranged in a circle, spinning to reveal. Game show feel, dramatic reveals. Creates anticipation. Good for dramatic moments but harder to read all letters at once.

**Pros:** Dramatic, game show feel, memorable
**Cons:** Harder to read, slower reveals, complex animation
**Best for:** Dramatic reveals, game show style

### Option C: Typewriter Reveal
**Description:** Letters appear one by one like typing. Creates suspense, builds tension. Each letter reveal is a micro-moment. Good for storytelling but slower overall.

**Pros:** Suspenseful, dramatic, storytelling potential
**Cons:** Slow, less time for guessing
**Best for:** Dramatic content, educational reveals

### Option D: Scattered Drop
**Description:** Letters fall from top of screen and scatter randomly. Physics-based animation, playful feel. Creates organic, unique layouts each round. Can be hard to read but very engaging.

**Pros:** Unique, engaging, organic feel, memorable
**Cons:** Can be hard to read, unpredictable layouts
**Best for:** Visual interest, unique branding, entertainment

# CARD 14: Tutorial Approach

## How should new players learn the game?

### Option A: No Tutorial - Learn by Playing (RECOMMENDED)
**Description:** Game is simple enough to figure out naturally. First round acts as implicit tutorial. Minimal instructions shown. Trust player intelligence. Reduces friction.

**Pros:** Zero friction, fast onboarding, respects intelligence
**Cons:** Some may be confused, no guidance
**Best for:** Simple games, experienced gamers

### Option B: Quick Interactive Tutorial
**Description:** 30-second interactive guide before first game. Shows controls, explains rules through play. Can be skipped. Balances guidance with speed.

**Pros:** Clear guidance, reduces confusion, skippable
**Cons:** Adds time, some may skip anyway
**Best for:** Complex games, new player retention

### Option C: First Game as Tutorial
**Description:** First round is guided with prompts and explanations. "This is how you guess" etc. Gradually removes prompts. Natural learning curve.

**Pros:** Natural learning, contextual, gradual
**Cons:** May feel hand-holdy, slower start
**Best for:** Casual players, educational approach

### Option D: Tips Overlay
**Description:** Optional tips button shows help anytime. Non-intrusive, available when needed. Doesn't force tutorial on experienced players. Good balance.

**Pros:** Available when needed, non-intrusive, optional
**Cons:** Players may not find it, extra UI element
**Best for:** Balancing guidance with freedom

# CARD 16: Anti-Cheat Strategy

## How will you prevent cheating?

### Option A: Server-Side Validation (RECOMMENDED)
**Description:** All game logic runs on server. Client only displays. Cannot cheat by manipulating client. Most secure, requires server infrastructure. Standard for competitive games.

**Pros:** Most secure, fair competition, standard approach
**Cons:** Requires server costs, more complex
**Best for:** Competitive integrity, fair play

### Option B: Client-Side with Obfuscation
**Description:** Game logic on client but obfuscated. Cheating possible but harder. Lower server costs, easier to implement. Less secure but good enough for casual play.

**Pros:** Lower costs, easier implementation, good enough for casual
**Cons:** Can be cracked, less secure
**Best for:** Casual play, budget constraints

### Option C: Community Reporting
**Description:** Players report suspicious behavior. Manual review of reports. Low-tech solution, community-driven. Can be slow, requires moderation. Good for small communities.

**Pros:** Low cost, community-driven, simple
**Cons:** Slow, requires moderation, can be abused
**Best for:** Small communities, low budget

### Option D: Accept Cheating
**Description:** Focus on fun, not competition. Cheating doesn't ruin experience for others. Simplest approach, no anti-cheat needed. Fine for casual, non-competitive games.

**Pros:** Simplest, no cost, focus on fun
**Cons:** May frustrate honest players, no competitive integrity
**Best for:** Casual play, fun-focused, non-competitive
